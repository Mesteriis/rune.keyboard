# Q6: historical source-token boundaries and future selector guard

All 9,000 targets in the retained historical word-ranking rows were matched against their original articles in the exact pinned Wikipedia shard. The audit checks every matching prefix/target occurrence. Thirteen rows have multiple source matches, but every match for each of those rows has the same standalone-token classification; none was assigned to an arbitrary occurrence. No article or target was missing.

The source boundaries show 8,948 standalone targets, 51 first components of hyphenated source tokens and one Cyrillic target fragment followed by an alphanumeric character from another alphabet. These 52 include the five previously traced unknown-head cases. They identify a historical selector boundary artifact, not evidence that the source authors intended the fragment as a standalone word. The original archived rows and historical reproduction remain untouched.

The 52 affected public source contexts were retained with attribution and registered in both permanent ledgers. They comprise 46 distinct full-token surfaces, of which 43 were absent from the earlier control surface exclusions, and 47 newly blocked normalized contexts. All 52 full surfaces are refused even under a changed prefix, and their contexts are refused by the lexical gate. The previous entries and incomplete coverage declarations remain unchanged. The [source audit receipt](2026-10-29-q6-wordrank-boundaries.json) binds the pinned shard, archived rows, source lock and audit; two source runs and two registry builds matched byte for byte. The tracked CI check revalidates the retained fixture and archive bindings without `build/`; it does not independently re-read the Wikipedia shard.

Four maintained selector paths for new proposals now reject a Cyrillic fragment adjacent to a hyphen, another letter or digit, underscore, or combining mark. The source-attachment helper independently refuses such a partial target, so a caller cannot bypass the check. This guard operates only for registry-checked proposals; explicit historical reproduction retains the original selector. Full historical regeneration matched all six refinement JSONL files, all seven word-ranking JSONL files and all seven additional-epoch JSONL files byte for byte, and the historical retrieval rows matched their original recorded SHA-256. Historical manifests differ in source/guard identity and are not claimed byte-identical. These checks do not qualify a new corpus or establish complete historical provenance.

Sixteen focused holdout tests, two boundary-audit tests and 20 preparation tests passed, including standalone, hyphenated, mixed-script, combining-mark and forged-source controls. Two disclosure tests passed. Full independent collector output remains gated on registry completeness and the separate text-corpus barrier; no APK, model, correction policy or device was changed.
